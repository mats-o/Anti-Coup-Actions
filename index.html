A Field Guide Updated May 2026

Defending the
2026 Election.

What's happening to American elections, who's tracking it, and what you can actually do about it.

For the first time in modern American history, the federal government itself has become a threat to the integrity of U.S. elections. The administration's strategy depends on confusion — a fog of executive orders, investigations, lawsuits, and disinformation that no single person can track in full.

This page is a starting point. It collects the most credible trackers monitoring the threat, and the most useful organizations to plug into if you want to do something about it. It is intentionally short. The point is to make the next step obvious.

Part two

Take action

Five categories, ordered by impact-per-hour.

Pick one and commit through November 2026. Sustained engagement with a single organization is dramatically more useful than crisis-driven spikes across many.

Category 01 Highest leverage

Become a paid poll worker

The integrity of elections lives or dies with the people physically running the precincts. Most local boards of elections are chronically short-staffed. You'll be paid, trained, and embedded inside the actual machinery on Election Day. Apply through your county or municipal Board of Elections.

Get started
  • EAC: Help America Vote — find your local officeeac.gov
  • Work Elections — apply by ZIP codeworkelections.com
  • Power the Polls — recruitment hubpowerthepolls.org
Category 02 High leverage

Volunteer with Election Protection

The nation's largest nonpartisan voter protection coalition, run by the Lawyers' Committee for Civil Rights Under Law. Volunteers staff the 866-OUR-VOTE hotline and serve as poll monitors. If you have a JD or are in law school, the legal volunteer track is especially needed.

Sign up
  • Election Protection volunteer signup866ourvote.org
  • We The Action — skilled volunteer matchingwetheaction.org
  • Protect The Vote 2026 — swing-state focusprotectthevote2026.org
Category 03 High $ leverage

Fund the litigation infrastructure

Most of the executive orders attacking elections have been blocked because lawyers were in the right courtroom at the right time. This is where money has the highest marginal impact. The Brennan Center, Protect Democracy, ACLU, and the Lawyers' Committee are doing the actual courtroom work that has so far succeeded.

Donate
  • Brennan Center for Justicebrennancenter.org
  • Protect Democracyprotectdemocracy.org
  • Lawyers' Committee for Civil Rightslawyerscommittee.org
  • Center for Election Innovation & Research (funds EOLDN, which legally protects election officials)electioninnovation.org
Category 04 Geographic leverage

Engage swing states from anywhere

The 2026 House and Senate margins will be decided in Arizona, Georgia, Michigan, Nevada, Pennsylvania, and Wisconsin. If you can't physically travel, phone-banking, postcard programs, and donations to swing-state grassroots groups all work. Mobilize aggregates events nationally; NOPE Neighbors curates vetted recommendations.

Find an opportunity
  • Mobilize — search by ZIP and causemobilize.us
  • NOPE Neighbors — curated, vetted recommendationsnopeneighbors.org
  • Vote Save Americavotesaveamerica.com
Category 05 Civic baseline

Engage your own state

State legislators have direct authority to strengthen election security, protect officials from intimidation, and clarify procedures. Even non-swing states matter — a strong state law in Maryland or California can become a model elsewhere. The Brennan Center publishes specific legislative recommendations.

Tools
  • Brennan Center: state legislator playbookbrennancenter.org
  • League of Women Voters — local chapterslwv.org
  • Common Cause — state affiliatescommoncause.org

Part three

Share & maintain

If this page is useful, send it to two people. The honest gap in this ecosystem is that nobody has built a single site combining threat-tracking and action — partly because the trackers are 501(c)(3) research groups that legally can't tell you what to do, and the action sites are 501(c)(4)s that don't do rigorous threat analysis. This page is a small attempt to bridge that.

Source code lives on GitHub. Fork it, fix it, fork it again. The links above will rot — please open issues or PRs when you find broken ones.

This page is a personal civic project, not affiliated with any organization listed above. All trackers and groups linked here are credibly nonpartisan or transparent about their orientation. Inclusion is editorial judgment, not endorsement.

Corrections welcome. The threat picture and the resource landscape both change weekly.
